    Evaluation

    The whole diagram, compared with the law

    What the diagram claims overall, what U.S. law provides, and what trusts can and cannot do.

    The claim

    The diagram depicts a path from public institutions, through a “public nexus” and the National Reform Protocol, to private equity and fully protected assets.

    What the law says

    No statute or court decision recognizes that path. Liability, taxation and creditor rights are set by specific laws, and a person's declared status or a trust's label does not change them.

    What trusts can legitimately do

    A properly drafted and funded trust can avoid probate, provide for management if you become incapacitated, and — in some irrevocable forms — limit creditor access within the limits state law sets.

    What trusts cannot do

    A trust cannot eliminate taxes you owe, place assets beyond the reach of courts, or shield property transferred to avoid existing creditors. The IRS treats trusts used to evade taxes as abusive.
